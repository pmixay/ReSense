## Interface and implementation

Node and launch defaults agree:

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `catchup_startup_step` | 0.2 s | Thin even short startup backlogs |
| `catchup_startup_max_lag` | 2.5 s | Startup recording-lag cutoff |
| `max_pending_frames` | 12 | Global Python pending-frame cap, across all inputs |
| `input_queue_depth` | 12 | DDS KEEP_LAST depth per subscription |
| `catchup_step` | 0.3 s | Later live catch-up step; 0 selects newest-only |
| `catchup_max_lag` | 5.0 s | Later live recording-lag cutoff |

Startup pressure is measured decode + detect + publish wall time divided by the estimated
sensor period. Cost uses an EMA (latest frame weight 0.2; first observation initializes it).
At pressure ≥ 0.8, the startup step increases by one sensor period, capped at 0.3 s.
A nanosecond-sized comparison tolerance makes the exact 0.8 boundary stable. At 10 Hz and
90 ms/frame this selects 0.3 s. A single waiting frame is processed immediately.

Explicit every-frame compatibility is still available. For the old 20 s / 201-frame
experiment the tests set `catchup_startup_step=0`, `catchup_startup_max_lag=20.1`, and
`max_pending_frames=256`; setting the step alone does not bypass the memory/lag bounds.

Pruning now considers **every contiguous input epoch**, including inputs behind a different
head of queue. It runs on the callback's initial message, on each DDS take, and before
processing. After thinning, excess oldest frames are evicted to enforce the global cap.
Every eviction immediately increments both total drops and received/skipped drops.
Skip credits transfer to retained successors, preventing double-counting when a later header
gap is accounted. Credits are counts keyed by retained messages, replacing the old discarded-
stamp list and its lossy truncation. Arrival metadata and queued-epoch markers are also keyed
only by retained messages and removed when those messages are processed or evicted.

Queued header epochs preserve their original boundary through thinning. In particular,
discarding the backwards first frame of a restarted bag cannot disguise that restart as an
ordinary forward gap. Continuity is checked before catch-up state is established, so even
a single-message new input starts its own epoch. Scene resets, mount restart rules, source
clock rejection, epoch progression, watchdog expiry, and STOP retention remain exercised by
the complete node suite.

Additional `status.node` telemetry:

- `pending_frames`: waiting frames, excluding the frame currently being processed;
- `max_pending_frames`: effective configured cap (at least one);
- `pending_overflow`: cumulative hard-cap evictions, a subset of received/skipped drops;
- `catchup_step_s`: effective catch-up step.

`catchup_skipped` now includes evictions before the first result and from abandoned queued
epochs. `dropped_frames` includes them immediately as well; a later inferred header gap adds
only missing frames not already counted. No skipped-record list is silently truncated.

## Deterministic acceptance evidence

stubs and the real `on_cloud`, `take_waiting`, `prune`, `process_next`, decode, continuity,
freshness, and publishing methods. Only detector work and clocks are deterministic stand-ins.
The simulated DDS handle releases frames at their scheduled publication times; source UTC is
that publication time, not the later take time. It delivers 10 s of 10 Hz headers in 1 s,
then continues live at 10 Hz for 3 s. Each processed frame costs 90 ms.
