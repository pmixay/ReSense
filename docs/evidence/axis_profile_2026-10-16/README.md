## 1. How the objects move: fixed in the tunnel, not riding with the train

The label distance of nine of the ten objects falls by about 1.5–2.0 m per frame (15–20 m/s, 55–70 km/h at 10 Hz):
they stand still in the tunnel and the train approaches them. The exception is `big_center`, which appears at 98.7 m
while the train is still slow (0.13 m per frame at first, 0.37 m per frame 120 frames later: the train is accelerating),
so its median step is lower. Consequence for the
`ego_veto` rule ([`../../ALGORITHM.md`](../../ALGORITHM.md)): on this recording the objects are not "carried along with
the train", so the rule does not suppress them; the shipped gate's 8 of 8 `STOP` agrees. Whether the hidden set is
generated the same way is not confirmed (the organizers did not answer the question directly).

**Checked 18.10 against the train's own motion:** ICP odometry on the real scans of Set O frames 0–799 (the judge's
`icp_odometry.py`, median fitness 0.98–0.99) gives the train's travel per frame. For every one of the ten objects the
label approach per frame equals the train's travel to within ±0.3 m/s (median), so they are static in the world, as
stated above ([`../synthetic_labels_2026-10-17/icp_setO_0-799.json`](../synthetic_labels_2026-10-17/icp_setO_0-799.json)).
The 16.10 synthetic bags differ: there the inserted objects move about 12.5 m/s toward the train
([`../synthetic_labels_2026-10-17/README.md`](../synthetic_labels_2026-10-17/README.md) "The inserted objects move").

Reading the first table:

* The median axis error is 0.1–0.35 m up to 80 m (90th percentile ≤ 0.5 m), 0.4–0.5 m at 80–150 m, and the tail grows: the 90th
  percentile exceeds the 1.05 m half-width of the gauge from about 120 m (1.55 m), reaches 3.3 m at 150–200 m, and is
  meaningless beyond 200 m (11.5 m: the axis is extrapolated past what the walls support). This is the
  "gauge does not follow the curve" the experts describe (live Q&A, 16.10), and it is the same range where
  [`BACKLOG.md`](../../BACKLOG.md) K1 / K2 measure `beyond_axis` and axis jitter.
* The raw per-frame axis moves by a median of 0.02–0.03 m per frame up to 100 m and 0.05–0.11 m beyond 100 m,
  with a 90th percentile of 0.29–0.38 m beyond 120 m: a far gauge edge that would visibly flicker if drawn from the
  raw fit. The live node smooths the axis in time ([`BACKLOG.md`](../../BACKLOG.md) K1: "yaw and curvature are re-fitted
  from the walls every frame and smoothed per time"), so its jitter is lower; K1 measures the live axis.
* Points: a 0.3 m cube has 1–2 returns beyond 80 m, 4 at 60–80 m and 9 at 40–60 m; the 2 m box has 16 at 200 m+, ~70
  at 100–120 m; the thin hanging object 2–4 at every range. A cluster-based detector cannot confirm a cube beyond
  ~60 m from one frame; only multi-frame accumulation (which needs a known speed) can use single returns. This bounds
  R1 / R2 physically and says that "300 m" is realistic only for large objects and only if the axis is right.

## 3. Caveats

* **The lateral is measured against the per-frame (unsmoothed) axis** of `scripts/label_fake_objects.py`
  (`estimate_track`), not the live node's smoothed axis: the jitter is an upper bound for the node.
* **Part of the axis error is the organizers' placement drift** (they say the objects "float" along the track,
  [`../../organizers/answers.md`](../../organizers/answers.md) §9), and the reference (8–25 m) assumes the axis is right
  there. Treat the error as an upper estimate of the axis error.
* Ten objects of one recording; an object needs at least 5 frames at 8–25 m to give a near reference (all ten have
  them; `thin_hanging` has 10).
* Distances are along X from the sensor; the bins hold different numbers of frames per object (see `n`).
