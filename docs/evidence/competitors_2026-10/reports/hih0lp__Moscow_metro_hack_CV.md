# hih0lp/Moscow_metro_hack_CV

## Verdict
Classical geometry (numpy+scipy, no learned weights), Python only, ~6k LOC incl. 29 experiment scripts. Rich, honest lab notebook (experiments/SUMMARY.md), but the detector itself is a narrow-corridor (+-0.6 m, +-1.2 m for strong clusters) cluster detector and is clearly behind ReSense on gauge handling, track model and false-alarm control. Claimed metrics are mostly on its own ray-cast synthetics or in-sample on one recording; the bag-of-organizers numbers are admitted "not blind" in the README. Little here that ReSense lacks; a few cheap eval/robustness ideas only.

## Pipeline
1. `pipeline.py:Processor.process`: auto forward-axis from far points (`orient.py:detect_forward`), crop |x-axis|<6 m, 0.3-215 m. Fixed axis `X_AXIS=-0.10` in sensor frame (no rail fit; rail-pair axis estimate tried and rejected as unstable).
2. `detector.py:floor_level` (5th percentile z in 4-12 m strip) + `estimate_floor_slope` (Theil-Sen over 6 m slabs 6-66 m, median of 15 frames, deadband) -> straighten cloud.
3. `detector.py:Detector.detect`: points 0.35-2.3 m above floor, range-banded clustering (grid-label / KD-tree), eps grows with range, per-cluster filters (extent, rail-like line, n >= 5.4e4/r^2 capped, top >= 0.5 m beyond 60 m), corridor test on centroid.
4. `bigblobs.py:BigObjectChannel` (24.09 addition): hard clustering, big (>=0.9x0.9 m), isolated (point-share of neighbourhood >= 0.6), explicit gauge +-1.1 m x 3.0 m, wall-midline curve straightening.
5. `background.py:BackgroundChannel` + moving-object channel in `tracker.py` (only when ego speed ~0 from track medians): voxel-occupancy frequency over 60 frames, anomaly = voxel occupied <=30 % of window.
6. `tracker.py:Tracker`: k-of-n (3 of 5) confirmation, strong clusters (n>=80) instantly, stability test for weak tracks, hold 5 frames; TTC/braking distance output (`pipeline.py`, assumptions a=1 m/s^2, reaction 1 s).

## Things done worse than ReSense / red flags
- Corridor is +-0.6 m (centroid test), +-1.2 m only for >=40-point clusters; organizers' gauge is 2.1 m. Low FA is bought by ignoring in-gauge edge objects (their own bag eval: edge-of-gauge big object 0 %, small edge 20 %, small on-rail 13 %, low 2x0.2 m 3 %).
- Fixed axis `X_AXIS=-0.10`, no rail fit, no curve handling in main detector (`curve=True` tripled FA, their note). ReSense track model is far stronger.
- Alarm-frame rates on the shipped recordings are high: `results/*.jsonl` (verified by counting): doubleT_platform 19.7 % (7 episodes / 345 fr), roundT_doubleT 58.7 %, roundT_pressureGate_roundT 22.8 %, squareT_platform_squareT_switch 6.8 % (5 episodes / 877 fr), 10.6 % on the 4th; "alarm" includes a 5-frame hold and the recordings contain real objects, so this is an upper bound, not comparable 1:1 to our episode counts.
- README quotes ray-cast results (person 70-90 % at 15-175 m) from its own synthetic; their own Round-6 analysis shows that benchmark was on-axis static objects and missed the real anomaly; labels of the real recording were changed three times in the journal (beam vs person), i.e. no trustworthy ground truth. Docker/ROS never run on the dev machine until late (README says built on macOS arm64 at the end).
- Everything tuned in-sample on `doubleT_obstacle` + 5 recordings; README has an explicit list of which parameters were fit where.

## Eval/data tooling worth borrowing
- `experiments/eval_perturb.py` as a template for whole-frame robustness sweep (idea 1).
- `experiments/eval_sections.py` / SUMMARY "sections": recall bucketed by axis-drift at 60 m (straight <0.5 m, gentle, sharp >1.5 m) - cheap stratification of our recall by curvature (ours stratifies by cause, not curvature).
- Honest per-parameter "what was fit on which data" table in README section "Что подбиралось на каких данных" - good practice to copy into our docs.
