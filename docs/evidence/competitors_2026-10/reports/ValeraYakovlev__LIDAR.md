# ValeraYakovlev/LIDAR

## Verdict
Classical geometry, no learning, no object classes: a tunnel-path model (rails near, walls far) with a rigid 81-717 body gauge swept along it, then voxel DBSCAN and "3 of 5 frames" confirmation. It is mature as a hackathon deliverable (ROS 2 node, Docker, ~78 ms/frame on M4, bit-exact regression checks). The repo is one squashed commit (no history), docs are Russian, and the knowledge base is long and unusually honest. Claimed metrics:

- Real person on rail at 55.5 m, 66 of 72 frames.
- Rail box in 92 of 125 frames (found only after the rule was written with the numbers visible).
- 0 confirmed false alarms on 5 clean real bags, except `roundT_doubleT` with 24 false frames.
- "Blind" mix3 table: 85/96/90/69/35 % recall by range, 0/999 false.

Trust is moderate to low as evidence of FPR or range:

- The only false-alarm data is ~2.3k real frames (~4 min). There is no 13 km ride, so there is no FPR per km.
- The mix3 numbers are not backed by any committed result file (`grep mix3 research/results` finds nothing).
- Dev/holdout splits are disciplined (by path length, tagged commits), but the holdout was consumed and later rules (low_rest_b0) were chosen after seeing the numbers. The authors say so themselves (knowledge.md §37).

## Pipeline
1. `rail_detection/views.py:rasterize` / `run_silhouette`: top-down grid, depth-normalised contrast, left/right edge per depth band.
2. `rail_detection/parallel_path.py:WallParallelTracker`: path as a curvature profile κ(s) (nodes every 10 m) plus pose. Walls are constant normal offsets from the path (<= 2 steps). Rails give the near path, walls give curvature to 150 m. Tukey-robust fit. Nodes are tied to the tunnel and shifted by -Δs per frame. Two starts per frame ("memory" and "fresh"); the cheaper one wins, with a capped prior and hysteresis.
3. `rail_detection/shift.py:ShiftMeter`: Δs from a density-profile correlation after subtracting the sensor-fixed pattern (mean of the last 10 profiles).
4. `rail_detection/roll.py` and `contrast_gauge.py`: roll from rail heads, rail-head plane, gauge frame (s, u, v).
5. `rail_detection/far_detect.py:vertical_profile`: far-field height profile from a side view (floor where visible, vault minus its height over the rails measured at 15-35 m).
6. `far_detect.py:gauge_mask`: body ±1.335 m, 0.10-3.65 m above the rail head, ±1.10 m below 0.5 m (contact-rail cut-out), shrunk by 0.008·(D-40) m on all sides.
7. `far_detect.py:clusters_far`: voxel 5 cm, DBSCAN in "angular" coordinates, threshold = 0.5 x expected beam hits on a 0.25 m object (>= 3 voxels).
8. `far_detect.py:TrackWatch`: every cluster is tracked in tunnel coordinates (S+dist); confirmed at 3 hits in 5 frames, tolerance growing with range. The nearest confirmed cluster is the answer.
9. The path limit is the reach of the walls, capped at 150 m. No infrastructure classification at all; no GO/CAUTION/STOP states, only detected / distance.

## Things done worse than ReSense / red flags
- No false-alarm control on a long ride. The FPR numbers cover ~2.3k real frames. Their own confirmed false frames on real data: `roundT_doubleT` 24 / 22 (mirror), and all of them from path error in the mouth. Our 13 km ride and the 11 switch episodes cannot be compared. Their detector shows 0 confirmed false on `squareT_platform_squareT_switch`, but the sample is short.
- The body gauge is 2.67 x 3.65 m, not the 2.1 x 3.0 m of the task, and there is no warning band. Their object-by-object ranges on `cloud_with_fake_obj` (2 x 2 centre 103 m, top 130 m, 0.3 m cube at centre 63 m, rail 59 m, edge 46-52 m) are therefore not comparable with ours (98 / 111.5 / 55.8 / 48 / 35 m): different gauge, different width, ranges taken as max confirmed distance.
- `cloud_with_fake_obj` objects are pasted in the sensor frame, so on a tilted tunnel low objects "float" with range (they measured +0.3-0.6 m at 60-90 m); their low-object ranges are inflated by this, as their own text says. The 5 cm hanging cord: found for only 2 frames at 50 m (ours: 14 of 20 frames from 30 m), so ReSense is better there.
- The 150 m path cap and the `D_MAX` raster cut off range; no hanging/overhead logic; no calibrated confidence; no health/FAULT handling; no ego_veto equivalent (they confirm by Δs consistency only, which degenerates when the train stands, §24).
- Labels of `cloud_with_fake_obj` were made by eye; ours are exact (appended points).

## Eval/data tooling worth borrowing
- Split by path length (not frames), commit/tag before the first edit, one holdout use, criteria written before measurement; the holdout was spent and the repo says so.
- Golden-reference checks of "same answer" after every optimisation (`research/exp_speed.py check/golden`, `.sha256` files), including a numpy vs numba bit-for-bit check. ReSense has `detector_freeze.py`, so only the habit matters.
- Mirror replay (idea 4) and the `/tf`-based truth path (idea 5).
- Rule: a result is compared only against a baseline that was re-measured on the same frames (they were burnt by file-name case clashes and different frame windows).
