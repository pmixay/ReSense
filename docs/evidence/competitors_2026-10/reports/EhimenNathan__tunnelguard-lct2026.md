# EhimenNathan/tunnelguard-lct2026

Paths below are relative to `src/tunnel_guard/tunnel_guard/` (core code) or the repo root (`tools/`, `docs/`). Repo is a single-commit shallow clone (no history to audit). Language of README/docs: Russian.

## Verdict
Hybrid: classical track-geometry front end (cross-section template + dense Gauss-Newton alignment of centreline and rail-height profiles, 2-rail Viterbi DP) plus a physics-rule stage and a small learned candidate scorer (LightGBM + CatBoost ensembles, JSON-exported trees evaluated in numpy). Mature, well documented, numba-accelerated, 15 unit tests.
Claimed metrics: 58-59/60 STOP frames on the one real obstacle recording (first STOP 55.5 m), 0 false STOP frames on 2287 empty frames, sealed-drive 1.8 false events/km, person STOP at 142-160 m (synthetic). The real-obstacle number is plausible but a single standing-train scene. The empty-frame numbers are in-sample for the deployed scorer, and the range numbers come from the authors' own ray-cast generator (the same generator trains the scorer). The authors flag this themselves (EXPERIMENTS.md sec. 3, 7, 8, 12); the README headline numbers do not repeat the caveats.

## Pipeline
1. `core/cloud.py` auto forward axis; crop (FOV 70 deg, min range), near-field decimation (x<25 m, every 3rd point). No intensity or ring use anywhere in the detector.
2. `core/geometry.py:GeometryEstimator.estimate`: rails 3-45 m by BEV max-height map, top-hat ridge (`_ridge_map`), pair evidence at 1.52 m gauge, Viterbi DP (`_track_dp`) gives axis, rail-head height and roll.
3. `geometry.py:CrossSectionTemplate`: near-field points (4-30 m) in track coordinates accumulate (EMA 0.85) into an occupancy image. Its truncated distance fields are the alignment target.
4. `geometry.py:_coarse_dp` + `_align`: global lateral-offset DP per 3-5 m slab to 300 m, then banded Gauss-Newton (numba `_gn_kernel`) refines `y_c(x)` and `z_r(x)` on a 1 m grid. Clothoid-like prior on curvature change, Kalman-style temporal prior, Tukey weights. Points inside the gauge core never steer the geometry. Per-node sigma_l, sigma_v come from information, a distance-growing systematic term and left/right wall disagreement.
5. `core/gauge.py:classify`: piecewise w(h) gauge (+-1.10..1.35 m, roof 3.25 m, so ~2.7 m wide, not 2.1 m). IN_GAUGE = gauge shrunk by 2 sigma(x). NEAR_GAUGE (CAUTION) = nominal gauge. `two_tier`: STOP only up to the range measured in this frame.
6. `core/cluster.py`: grid in (s, l) with range-growing cell, connected components, split by vertical gaps > 2.5 beam spacings. Rule flags in `core/detector.py:process`: gravity (>60 m must be floor-supported), shape (>40 m needs >=1.2 beam spacings or >=15 points), containment (wall evidence beyond the cluster), shell attachment.
7. `core/features.py` (38 physical features) -> `core/scorer.py` router: floor expert (threshold 0.425) or hanging expert (h_min >= 0.5 m, threshold 0.45). Per-track margin smoothing. Below 30 m the rules decide. Shell clusters are a hard veto.
8. `core/tracker.py` 3-of-5 confirmation, alpha-beta closing speed. `detector.py:_carried_along` ego-motion veto from `core/adapt.py` odometry (wall-signature cross-correlation).

## Things done worse than ReSense / red flags
- Gauge is ~2.7 m wide at body height (+-1.35 m), not the 2.1 m of the case. On the organizer set, objects 5 and 7 (declared outside the gauge) give STOP at 25 and 29 m. The authors guess the organizers' gauge is about +-1.15 m from the lidar axis and chose not to fit it. ReSense uses 2.1 m.
- Sealed-drive protocol is weaker than it looks. The same drive's t<900 s part trains the negatives. The score swings 11/7/2 events across three seeds. The final router is chosen after seeing dataset 3 and is worse than v4 on the sealed block (23 vs 17 frames). The "0 false frames on 2287" figure is in-sample for the deployed scorer (README says so only in `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`). Their rate (1.8 events/km, last 2.8 km only, wide gauge) is not comparable with our 2.3 STOP episodes/km on 13 km at the 2.1 m gauge.
- Range table counts recall only over "physically visible" cases, first second of an approach, short-warm-up geometry. It is not the stable-STOP-per-start protocol we use (our 80 m = 8/15 vs their 100 %). Do not compare. Section 8 concedes that on the real corridor most 140-220 m positions get no return.
- Latency: README 56-70 ms median, p95 ~102 ms (above the 100 ms frame period); dataset-2 summary shows median 138 ms, p95 288 ms, max 1.8 s (measured on a busy laptop). Pure numpy/numba, no C++ kernels.
- README and docs disagree slightly: recall 96.7 % (58/60) in the README vs 98.3 % (59/60) in EXPERIMENTS. Cause is seed variation, but it shows the holdout number moves by one frame. Dataset-2 was first misread as "staged obstacles" (EXPERIMENTS sec. 11), then corrected (sec. 12). `tools/results/` named in the HEAD commit is absent from the checkout.
- The `shell` hard veto is still in the hot path (refined: needs floor contact h_min < 0.8 m, height >= 2.5 m, shell continuity). Same residual risk we recorded: floor-to-roof objects (pole, standing train) can be hidden.

## Eval/data tooling worth borrowing
- Self-labelling by drive-through (`core/adapt.py:ClutterCalibrator.observe`, `tools/selflabel.py`, `tools/run_drive.py`): an alarm at tunnel coordinate X = x_train + s is proven false once the train passes X by >=3 m. It needs no human labels. Useful for triaging unlabeled test-stand rides, and for auto-negatives. The online adaptive threshold built on it showed no effect (EXPERIMENTS 12.8).
- Grouped protocol: leave-one-recording-out with the threshold chosen on training groups only at a false-alarm budget (`tools/train_scorer_v3.py`, `tools/weight_sweep.py`, `tools/ablation.py`: removes one component at a time and reruns empty frames, real holdout and ray-cast people/boxes at 80/120/160 m).
- Range budget analysis from the sensor manual (`tools/range_budget.py`, `tools/diag_range.py`, `config/pandar128_channels.csv`): shows that in these tunnels the corridor is visible to ~120-160 m and no return exists beyond ~209 m. Useful to set a realistic range ceiling and a `report_range_cap` (210 m).
- `tools/convert.py` streams rosbag2 SQLite out of the .zst archive without unpacking into range-image caches (handles the SQLite lock-byte page); `tools/export_bag.py` writes a ROS 2 bag from the cache. `tools/replica_ds3.py` rebuilds the organizers' 10-object sequence in real rays.
